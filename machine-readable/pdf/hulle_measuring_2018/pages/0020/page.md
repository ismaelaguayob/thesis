Region Wegener and Liebig (1995, 2010) showed that, on the whole, people living in
East Germany (former German Democratic Republic, GDR) are more likely to lean more
toward the equality principle than people living in West Germany. This finding has stood
the test of time, despite a slightly increasing convergence between East and West Germany
between 1990 and 2010 (Wegener and Liebig 2010; Gerlitz et al. 2012). Our analyses
using the LINOS-1 dataset confirmed this particular difference in attitudes between the two
parts of Germany: individuals from the new federal states had a significantly greater
preference for equality (M = 2.89, SD = 1.13) than did individuals from the old federal
states (M = 2.74, SD = 1.07), t(4461) = -3.64, p = 0.000 (see Table 10). This differ-
ence is explained by the fact that socio-structural conditions have an influence on attitude
formation and attitude change. It can be expected that the more the conditions in East and
West Germany converge in the future, the sooner the attitudes will converge as well
(Wegener and Liebig 2010). Another finding is that people living in West Germany have a
higher preference for the entitlement principle (M = 1.97, SD = 0.85) compared to people
who live in East Germany (M = 1.90, SD = 0.81), t(4461) = , p = 0.021.
Age Since older people are more likely to have health problems, they are more likely to
be in need. Therefore, we expect that support for the need principle increases with age. A
one-way ANOVA revealed significant differences in support for need between our three
considered age groups [F(2,4460) = 11.88, p = 0.000]. Indeed a positive age effect exists:
a Tukey post hoc test provided evidence that the youngest age group (B29 years) has a
significantly lower preference for need (M = 4.54, SD = 0.51) compared to the inter-
mediate age group (30–44 years) (M = 4.59, SD = 0.50, p = 0.019) and the oldest age
group (C34 years) (M = 4.63, SD = 0.49, p = 0.000).9
